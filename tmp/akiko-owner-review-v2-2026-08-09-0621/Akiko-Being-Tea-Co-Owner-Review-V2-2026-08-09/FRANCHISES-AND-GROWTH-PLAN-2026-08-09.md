# Akiko / Being Tea Co. — Franchises and Growth Plan

**Date:** August 9, 2026  
**Status:** Approval-ready local operating plan. It does not authorize publishing or external scheduling.

## Portfolio balance

- **60% proven/low-risk:** tea fundamentals, controlled brewing, opened-leaf observation, brewing logs.
- **25% adjacent:** source literacy, sensory practice, audience Q&A.
- **15% bold:** fictional-host versus brand-only controls, Between Steeps rituals, unusual visual storytelling.

No series scales merely because it looks beautiful. It must improve qualified reach, follows per 1,000 qualified views, return behavior, owned-audience conversion, or production efficiency.

## 1. Change One Thing

**Audience promise:** Diagnose a cup without changing the entire recipe.  
**Hook patterns:** “If the cup is bitter, do not change everything.” “One cup. One variable.” “Keep three things fixed.”  
**Episode structure:** Problem → baseline recipe → one variable → matched comparison → human-recorded observation → next test.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Three matched cups or two side-by-side brews; timer click, kettle, pour, pencil; clean overhead plus one close liquid shot.  
**Duration tests:** 15–25 seconds, 35–50 seconds, and a 90–150 second Facebook explanation.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram carousel/Reel; Facebook full method and source; X one-variable rule plus compact thread.  
**Production time:** 60–120 minutes when real brewing is recorded; 30–45 minutes for a no-result explainer.  
**Required assets:** Scale, thermometer/kettle, matched vessels, timer, leaf, water, human operator log.  
**Natural CTA:** “Save this for the next cup that goes wrong.”  
**Why people return:** The framework can solve many brewing problems and naturally creates sequels.  
**Monetization fit:** Later—tested scales, kettles, vessels, printable log, or beginner kit; never before real human use and clear disclosure.  
**Risks:** Changing two variables, fabricated results, presenting preference as universal.  
**Pilot episodes:** Lower/baseline/higher temperature; shorter/baseline/longer time; less/baseline/more leaf.

## 2. One Plant, Many Paths

**Audience promise:** Understand tea categories through processing rather than memorized prestige language.  
**Hook patterns:** “Six familiar names can begin with one species.” “Color is not the whole category.” “The leaf changed before the water did.”  
**Episode structure:** One plant → one processing decision → visible leaf change → cup implication → source.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Six small leaf fields, simple hands, paper labels added in platform-native graphics rather than generated imagery; dry leaf sound.  
**Duration tests:** 20–40 seconds and 5–7-slide carousels.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram saveable carousel; Facebook deeper processing explanation; X one distinction with source link.  
**Production time:** 45–90 minutes.  
**Required assets:** Correctly identified tea samples, source references, overhead surface, rights-cleared labels/graphics.  
**CTA:** “Which processing step should we slow down next?”  
**Return/share reason:** Builds a beginner curriculum with clear continuation.  
**Monetization fit:** Beginner course or sampler only after sourcing and actual human testing.  
**Risks:** Oversimplification, category absolutism, incorrect sample identification.  
**Pilots:** Oxidation is not fermentation; rolled versus twisted leaf; what roasting changes and what it does not prove.

## 3. Read the Opened Leaf

**Audience promise:** Learn what a brewed leaf can reveal—and what it cannot.  
**Hook patterns:** “The leaf after brewing still has something to say.” “Look before judging.” “A torn leaf is a clue, not a verdict.”  
**Episode structure:** Dry leaf → brewed leaf → three observable features → bounded interpretation → uncertainty.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Macro leaf, white tray, water sheen, stem/bud/edge details, minimal narration.  
**Duration tests:** 12–20 seconds, 30–45 seconds, and macro photo carousel.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram macro/save; Facebook explanation of limits; X one observation and one non-claim.  
**Production time:** 30–60 minutes plus brewing.  
**Assets:** Real correctly identified leaf, macro lens/phone, white tray, source card.  
**CTA:** “After your next cup, open one leaf.”  
**Return/share reason:** Tactile visual novelty plus a repeatable ritual.  
**Monetization fit:** Magnifier, tray, teaware, or workshop only after trust.  
**Risks:** Treating appearance as a quality grade or cultivar proof.  
**Pilots:** Edge and bruising; bud/stem/leaf composition; rolled oolong before and after infusion.

## 4. Akiko Checks the Claim

**Audience promise:** Separate what a source says from what a caption wants it to say.  
**Hook patterns:** “The study did not test that.” “Traditional use is not the same as clinical evidence.” “This headline skipped a step.”  
**Episode structure:** Claim → source type → what was actually measured → what remains unknown → safe wording.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Quiet desk, source card, highlighter, one sentence at a time; no fake journal pages.  
**Duration tests:** 30–60 seconds, 5-slide carousel, and 3-post X thread.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram visual claim check; Facebook citations and nuance; X concise correction with direct source.  
**Production time:** 90–180 minutes including research and human review.  
**Assets:** Primary source, archived citation, plain-language summary, specialist review for health claims.  
**CTA:** “Send a claim you want sourced; do not send personal medical questions.”  
**Return/share reason:** High trust and private sharing when misinformation is common.  
**Monetization fit:** Newsletter, research guide, education partnerships—not supplement sales.  
**Risks:** Medical advice, overstating study quality, antagonistic debunking, defamation.  
**Pilots:** “Detox tea”; tea and hydration; what water studies can and cannot show.

## 5. Five Passes Through a Cup

**Audience promise:** A simple Being Tea Co. attention practice: look, smell, sip, feel, wait.  
**Hook patterns:** “Do not decide at the first sip.” “One cup, five passes.” “The finish arrives after the swallow.”  
**Episode structure:** Name the five passes → demonstrate the observer's action → invite the follower to record one word → state that it is a house practice, not an industry standard.  
**Visual/audio signature:** One cup, close face/hands without pretending Akiko physically tastes, steam, paper, five clean cuts.  
**Duration tests:** 20–35 seconds and a saveable single-page guide.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram ritual Reel; Facebook full explanation; X five-word mnemonic.  
**Production time:** 45–75 minutes.  
**Assets:** Cup, human operator, blank log, original framework disclosure.  
**CTA:** “Record one word after the finish.”  
**Return/share reason:** A calming repeatable ritual that improves sensory vocabulary.  
**Monetization fit:** Journal/download, tasting workshop, Between Steeps.  
**Risks:** Presenting the framework as validated science or inherited ceremony.  
**Pilots:** One green tea; one roasted oolong; the same tea as it cools.

## 6. The Cup Keeps Notes

**Audience promise:** Build a personal brewing guide from evidence in your own kitchen.  
**Hook patterns:** “Your next cup starts with one line.” “Memory edits the recipe.” “Write the variable, not a poem.”  
**Episode structure:** Recipe line → one observation → next change → accumulated pattern after several sessions.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Notebook, pencil, timer, cup ring, quiet paper sound, consistent one-line template.  
**Duration tests:** 10–20 seconds, static template, monthly recap carousel.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram save/download; Facebook example and discussion; X one-line log prompt.  
**Production time:** 20–45 minutes.  
**Assets:** Original template, landing page/download, example clearly labeled illustrative.  
**CTA:** “Keep this line for the next session.”  
**Return/share reason:** Practical utility improves with repetition.  
**Monetization fit:** Free lead magnet first; later journal or membership archive.  
**Risks:** Arbitrary guarantees, collecting unnecessary personal data, turning notes into universal prescriptions.  
**Pilots:** Temperature log; water comparison log; “what I change next” line.

## 7. Tea, Tisane, or Something Else?

**Audience promise:** Use precise names without shaming familiar language.  
**Hook patterns:** “Peppermint is a wonderful drink. It is not *Camellia sinensis*.” “Precision is not a purity test.” “What plant is actually in the cup?”  
**Episode structure:** Common label → organism/plant part → preparation → evidence/safety boundary → respectful usage note.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Clearly separate botanicals, neutral background, plant/source labels in graphics, no apothecary mysticism.  
**Duration tests:** 20–45 seconds and 4–6-slide carousel.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram comparison; Facebook botanical/source depth; X one precise definition.  
**Production time:** 60–120 minutes including identification and safety review.  
**Assets:** Correctly sourced botanicals, authoritative plant references, specialist check for safety statements.  
**CTA:** “Check the ingredient before repeating the claim.”  
**Return/share reason:** Clears common confusion while respecting everyday speech.  
**Monetization fit:** Botanical reference guide only with expert review; no health promises.  
**Risks:** Plant misidentification, medical implications, interaction advice, gatekeeping.  
**Pilots:** Peppermint; rooibos; chaga as fungus rather than plant/tea.

## 8. Between Steeps

**Audience promise:** One thoughtful question to carry between cups and people.  
**Hook patterns:** “A question for the quiet between steeps.” “Ask this before the kettle returns.” “There is no correct answer.”  
**Episode structure:** One original prompt → ten seconds of quiet/process → invitation to answer privately or in comments → optional anonymous theme recap.  
**Visual/audio signature:** Cup, card, steam, room tone, restrained red accent; Akiko introduces but does not simulate intimate lived memories.  
**Duration tests:** Static card, 10–15 second Reel, monthly live discussion if approved.  
**Platform adaptations:** Instagram visual prompt; Facebook longer community story; X direct question.  
**Production time:** 20–45 minutes plus moderation.  
**Assets:** Rights-controlled prompt deck, permissions for any shared response, interest-list landing page.  
**CTA:** “Keep the answer, share it with someone, or leave one line.”  
**Return/share reason:** Private sharing and a weekly ritual beyond tea technique.  
**Monetization fit:** Free sampler → interest list → finished deck or membership only when product state is truthful.  
**Risks:** Engagement bait, therapy-like disclosures, privacy, crisis disclosures, claiming an unfinished product is for sale.  
**Pilots:** “What did you hurry past this week?” “What changed after you waited?” “Which ordinary object carries a family story?”

- Allocate 60% of output to the top two franchises, 25% to one adjacent series, 15% to a bold experiment.
- Add one human-run measured comparison weekly if production truthfully supports it.
- Test a 60–150 second Facebook lesson and a 35–60 second Instagram Reel from the same source session.
- Publish the first monthly correction/learning ledger.

## Days 61–90

- Retire series that cannot beat the account median after four comparable episodes.
- Create a pinned beginner playlist/path across the top-performing lessons.
- Invite two qualified contributors for sourced Q&A or co-created education.
- Test a low-risk live session only if identity disclosure, operator, moderation, capacity, and escalation are ready.

## Final stretch through December 31

- 70% proven winners, 20% adjacent tests, 10% breakout experiments.
- Recut no content by downloading from another platform; return to clean source files.
- Pursue newsletter swaps, podcast/newsletter contributions, legitimate event tie-ins, and collaborator reposts with permission.
- Reforecast weekly; protect trust and production sustainability even if stretch probability falls.

## Production workflow

1. **Question:** identify one useful audience problem.
2. **Evidence:** archive primary sources and classify fact, tradition, experience, hypothesis, or experiment.
3. **Risk:** route health/culture/legal/rights claims to human review.
4. **Script:** one hook, one distinction, one action, one limit, one CTA.
5. **Capture:** shoot/generate clean masters and record provenance/releases.
6. **Native edit:** create separate Instagram, Facebook, and X treatments.
7. **Accessibility:** captions, alt text, contrast, readable graphics, transcript where needed.
8. **Approval:** owner reviews content, disclosure, destination, crop, and risk.
9. **Stage:** MyPersonas records approved state, platform map, dates, and duplicate check.
10. **Publish/verify:** only after explicit authority; preserve provider IDs and public URL.
11. **Measure:** capture 24-hour, seven-day, and 28-day results.
12. **Learn:** scale, revise, or stop; add reusable parts to evergreen reserve.

## Collaboration and earned-distribution plan

### Priority collaborators

1. Tea educators with verifiable credentials or clearly scoped lived expertise.
2. Independent tea shops and importers willing to document sourcing and commercial relationships.
3. Ceramicists and tool makers for process/permission-based education.
4. Food-science, plant-science, or sensory researchers for bounded questions.
5. Slow-living and beverage creators whose audiences value process over hype.
6. Tea newsletters, podcasts, festivals, libraries, and community groups.

### Outreach rule

- Maximum five personalized approaches per week.
- Lead with a concrete contribution, not “collab?”
- State that Akiko is fictional/AI-generated and human-directed.
- Ask for permission, credit preference, link, embargo, and reuse terms.
- No mass comments, mention spam, engagement trades, or pressure.
- Record declined/no-response outcomes without repeated solicitation.

### Earned-distribution offers

- A source-checked beginner graphic the collaborator may repost with credit.
- A short guest answer where the contributor speaks only within expertise.
- A co-run controlled brewing comparison with shared raw method/results.
- A newsletter exchange around a single educational question.
- A local event recap only when attendance and permission are real.

## Owned-audience plan

### Funnel

`native lesson → Start Here page → one-line brewing log → three-email beginner sequence → weekly field note → Between Steeps interest list`

### Start Here page

- One-sentence promise and fictional/AI disclosure above the fold.
- Four choices: Start Here, Brewing Guides, Brewing Log, Between Steeps.
- No pop-up before the visitor can understand the offer.
- Privacy statement, unsubscribe path, and no data beyond what the email service needs.

### First three emails

1. **Welcome:** identity/disclosure, one-variable rule, log download.
2. **Practice:** tea versus tisane and one plant/many paths.
3. **Return:** three-cup comparison and invitation to reply with one observation.

### Affiliate-link plan

- Do not add speculative or application-only programs.
- Record program, acceptance date, destination, commission, human product-use evidence, disclosure, and expiration.
- Put a plain disclosure before or next to the recommendation.
- Provide a non-affiliate alternative when practical.
- Pause any offer producing complaints, unclear tracking, or pressure to distort education.

## Evergreen reserve

Maintain at least 14 days of approved low-risk material:

- Four tea-definition posts.
- Three Change One Thing explainers without claimed results.
- Two opened-leaf observation prompts.
- Two brewing-log reminders.
- Two Between Steeps prompts.
- One disclosure/correction-process explainer.

Reserve items still require a destination, freshness, duplicate, and policy check before publication.

## Burnout prevention

- **Tier 1, 20–45 minutes:** X insight, Story, notebook still, prompt card.
- **Tier 2, 45–120 minutes:** carousel, sourced photo lesson, caption adaptations.
- **Tier 3, 2–4 hours:** controlled brew video, claim check, collaboration.
- Cap Tier 3 at two per week until production data shows room.
- Batch props and scene capture, not interchangeable captions or synthetic images.
- Keep one no-publish day per week.
- If production exceeds the weekly cap twice, cut volume before lowering sourcing or originality.
- Track qualified follows and signups per production hour.
