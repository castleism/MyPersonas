Reading the badges

What kind of support an entry rests on

A badge does not rate how true something is. It says where the backing comes from — including when there is no factual claim to back at all.

Health-agency source

The entry summarises material published by a national health body such as NIH's NCCIH or NHLBI. Educational context, not a verdict about you.

Methodology reference

The entry teaches a research-methods idea drawn from a published methodological guide, and explicitly does not turn personal observation into clinical evidence.

Regulatory guidance

The entry explains what regulators say advertisers and labels may claim — FDA labeling rules and FTC substantiation standards. It is not legal advice.

Editorial promise · no efficacy claim

The entry states how this project intends to behave. It makes no claim about any product, ingredient or health outcome, so there is nothing to cite.

Introduction / charter · no factual claim

Framing, identity and values. Again: nothing empirical is asserted, so no source is owed.

The standard

What has to be true before something is published here

  • The claim is stated precisely enough to be checked — no "science-backed" without saying which science.
  • The source is read, not just the headline, and its limitations travel with it.
  • Uncertainty is shown rather than smoothed away, and a null or inconvenient result is kept.
  • General education is distinguished from individual guidance, every time.
  • A human reviews health-adjacent content before it goes out; the fictional and AI-assisted nature is disclosed with it.

A disclaimer at the bottom cannot repair a misleading overall impression at the top. If those two ever conflict here, the impression is the thing that was wrong.

Hard limits

What this project will never do

  • Claim Cillian personally used, tested, reacted to or benefited from any product — he is fictional and has no body, licence or history to draw on.
  • Diagnose, prescribe, or tell anyone what to take, how much, or what to combine.
  • Advise on medication changes, interactions, or how to manage an adverse event.
  • Call a supplement safe, effective or "clinically proven" without precise scope and qualified review.
  • Fabricate a reviewer, testimonial, citation, lab result or app capability.
  • Hide an affiliate, sponsor or gifted-product relationship — there are none at present, and any future one will be disclosed with the content it touches.
  • Target minors, or trade on anyone's addiction, eating disorder, anxiety, depression, disability or desperation.

Questions involving symptoms, medicines, doses, pregnancy, mental health or an adverse event belong with a qualified clinician or pharmacist — never with a comment section, and never automated here.
